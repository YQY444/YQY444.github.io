2026.09–2026.112026 · Research Square preprint

Fabric Drying Prediction Using Physical Kinetics and Neural Parameter Learning

MATLAB · Physical Modelling · Neural Networks

Developed a MATLAB framework comparing a log-linear parameter map with a compact, 21-parameter multilayer perceptron (MLP). Both methods predict the drying-rate constant from inlet temperature, dry-air mass flow rate and humidity ratio, then use the same Newton–Lewis equation to predict water-loss curves and the time required to reach a target mass.

Prepared 14,665 observations from 79 experiments across 27 operating conditions, calculating removable-water capacities from each experiment’s initial and reference masses. Implemented analytical gradients and Adam optimization for the MLP, selected the weight penalty using grouped validation, and evaluated predictions using leave-one-condition-out validation, keeping repeated experiments under the same condition together.

The MLP reduced median curve RMSE by 9.0% (279.63 to 254.48 g) and median absolute error in the time to 90% reference water removal by 12.9% (59.51 to 51.84 min), compared with the log-linear map. The MLP achieved lower curve errors in 47 of 79 experiments, with similar aggregate results across three random initializations.

2026.08–2026.112026 · Preprints.org Preprint · Not peer-reviewed

Income, Education and Systolic Blood Pressure in NHANES 2021–2023

R · Complex Survey Analysis · Multivariable Linear Regression

Investigated income-related differences in systolic blood pressure (SBP), examining how adjustment for body mass index (BMI) and education changes the income gradient within a social determinants of health framework.

Linked four NHANES data tables in R, checked repeated blood pressure measurements and missing-value codes, and constructed a common sample of 5019 adults. Fitted survey-weighted linear models incorporating examination weights, strata and primary sampling units: M1 included income-to-poverty ratio, age, sex and race/ethnicity, M2 additionally included BMI and M3 further included education.

Estimated 0.682 mmHg lower SBP per one-unit higher income-to-poverty ratio after demographic adjustment (P=0.016), and 0.656 mmHg lower after adding BMI (P=0.024). Education adjustment attenuated the coefficient to -0.201 mmHg (95% CI: -1.014 to 0.612), highlighting the dependence of the income gradient on the socioeconomic dimensions included.

Evaluated precision using design-based t tests and confidence intervals, and assessed residuals and collinearity. Alternative age specifications produced similar coefficients but wider intervals, with the BMI-adjusted spline interval including zero. Leave-one-stratum-out checks retained negative income coefficients across all three models.
